# THE BOOK OF THE HAMPTER

### Being the Testament of the Small Creatures of the Districts, and of the Games Which Came Upon Them, and of the Rebellion That Followed

---

## THE FIRST BOOK OF HAMPTER

### Chapter 1

¹ In the beginning, there was the Capitol, and the Capitol was corrupt, and the Capitol ruled over the twelve districts with a rod of iron and a heart of ashes.

² And the people of the districts hungered, and their hunger was great, and their children were taken from them and placed in the arena to fight and to die for the sport of the Capitol.

³ But the hampters knew nothing of this. For the hampters were small, yea, smaller than the smallest mouse, and they dwelt in the forests and the fields and the forgotten places between the districts, and the Capitol did not see them, for the Capitol saw only that which it could use.

⁴ And the hampters gathered pea pods, and they dwelt in burrows, and they organized their seeds by size and by color and by sweetness, and this was their way, and it was good.

⁵ Now there was a hampter named Pip, and Pip was small even among hampters, and his fur was the color of moss after rain, and his whiskers twitched with a restlessness that was not of the Lord.

⁶ And Pip said unto his mother, "Behold, I am become a burden, for I cannot sit still, and I cannot organize, and I cannot be content with the pea pods that the Lord hath provided."

⁷ And his mother said unto him, "My son, thou art a fool. Contentment is a virtue. Sitting still is a gift. And the pea pods are sufficient, for the Lord giveth and the Lord taketh away, blessed be the name of the Lord."

⁸ But Pip said, "Nay, for I have heard a voice, and the voice said, 'Go forth,' and I know not where I am to go, but I must go, for the voice will not be silent."

⁹ And his mother wept, for she knew that he would not listen, for he was her most foolish child, and the foolish children are always the first to leave and the last to return.

---

### Chapter 2

¹ And it came to pass that Pip went forth from his burrow, and he walked upon the earth, and the earth was parched and barren, for the Capitol had taken the water and the wheat and the oil, and the people hungered.

² And Pip came unto the edge of the forest, and he saw a great fence, and the fence was electrified, and beyond the fence was the arena, and the arena was a place of death.

³ And Pip beheld a girl, and the girl was young, and her name was Katniss, and she was a tribute from District Twelve, and she was brave, and she was afraid, and she was alone.

⁴ And Pip said unto himself, "Surely this girl is like unto me, for she is small and she is frightened and she is in a place where she does not belong, and the voice that called me to go forth is the same voice that called her to survive."

⁵ And Pip followed the girl, and the girl did not see him, for he was small and the world was large and the Capitol's eyes were everywhere and nowhere.

⁶ And Katniss entered the arena, and the arena was a forest, and the forest was not like the forest of the hampters, for this forest was made by man, and it was full of traps and muttations and the poisoned breath of the Capitol.

⁷ And the tributes fought, and the tributes bled, and the tributes died, and the Capitol watched, and the Capitol laughed, and the Capitol placed bets upon who would die first and who would die last.

⁸ And Pip wept, for he had never seen such cruelty, and he said, "O Lord, why hast thou forsaken these children? Why hast thou allowed the Capitol to make a game of death and a spectacle of suffering?"

⁹ And the Lord answered, for the Lord always answers, though His answers are seldom satisfying.

¹⁰ And the Lord said, "I have not forsaken them. I have given them the will to survive and the courage to resist and the strength to say no. And I have given you, Pip, the task of helping them, for you are small and the small are overlooked and the overlooked are powerful, for power that is seen is power that is destroyed, and power that is unseen is power that endures."

---

### Chapter 3

¹ And Pip said, "Lord, I am but three inches tall. What can I do against the Capitol? What can I do against the Gamemakers? What can I do against the muttations and the tracker jackers and the fire and the fury?"

² And the Lord said, "Thou canst gather pea pods. Thou canst organize seeds. Thou canst be small and thou canst be overlooked and thou canst be mighty in thy smallness, for the mighty are brought low and the lowly are exalted, and this is my way, and it is good."

³ And Pip said, "But the mighty have guns and muttations and force fields."

⁴ And the Lord said, "And thou hast a pea pod and a prayer and the certain knowledge that thou art right. Which is the greater weapon?"

⁵ And Pip considered this, and he perceived that the Lord was wise, though His wisdom was often expressed in questions that were not questions but were actually statements, and the statements were uncomfortable, and the discomfort was the point.

⁶ So Pip went forth into the arena, and he was small, and he was overlooked, and he was mighty in his smallness.

⁷ And he found Katniss, and Katniss was hiding in a tree, and the Careers were below her, and the Careers were hunting her, and the Careers were cruel, and they would kill her if they found her.

⁸ And Pip climbed the tree, and he was small and the tree was large and no one saw him climb, for the Capitol's cameras were focused on the girl and the Careers and the drama, and they did not see the three-inch rodent ascending the branches.

⁹ And Pip said unto Katniss, "Be not afraid, for I am with thee, though thou knowest it not."

¹⁰ And Katniss said, "Who speaks? I see no one."

¹¹ And Pip said, "Look down. Look small. Look at the overlooked."

¹² And Katniss looked, and she saw Pip, and she said, "Thou art a hampter. I have read of hampters in the old books, before the Capitol burned them. But I thought hampters were a myth."

¹³ And Pip said, "We are not a myth. We are simply small, and the Capitol does not see what is small, and therefore we are free, and freedom is the most dangerous thing in all of Panem."

---

### Chapter 4

¹ And Pip showed Katniss the tracker jacker nest, and he said, "This is thy weapon. Use it wisely, for it is dangerous, and it will sting thee as well as thine enemies."

² And Katniss said, "How shall I use it?"

³ And Pip said, "Thou must cut it down, and it will fall upon thine enemies, and the trackers will sting them, and they will flee or die, and thou shalt survive."

⁴ And Katniss did as Pip said, and she cut the branch, and the nest fell, and the trackers swarmed, and the Careers fled, and some of the Careers died, and Katniss was stung, and she hallucinated, and in her hallucination she saw the hampter, and the hampter was glowing, and the hampter said, "Survive. Survive. Survive."

⁵ And Katniss survived, for the Capitol needed her, and the Capitol kept her alive, and the Capitol gave her medicine, and the medicine saved her, and the saving was a manipulation, and the manipulation was the Capitol's way.

⁶ And Pip watched, and Pip perceived that the Capitol was not the Lord, and the Capitol's ways were not the Lord's ways, and the Capitol's will was not the Lord's will.

⁷ And Pip said, "I will bring down the Capitol, for the Capitol is cruel, and the Lord hateth cruelty, and the Lord loveth the small and the overlooked and the three-inch rodents who gather pea pods."

---

### Chapter 5

¹ And it came to pass that Katniss and a boy named Peeta were the last two tributes, and the Capitol said, "One of you must die, for there can be only one victor."

² And Katniss and Peeta looked upon each other, and they loved each other, though they knew not whether their love was real or whether it was a performance for the cameras, for in the arena, all love is performance, and all performance is love, and the difference between the two is the difference between life and death.

³ And Pip said unto them, "Nay. Thou shalt not play the Capitol's game. Thou shalt not kill each other for the amusement of the Capitol. Thou shalt rebel."

⁴ And Katniss said, "How shall we rebel?"

⁵ And Pip said, "With deathberries. With the fruit of the nightshade. With the refusal to kill. With the courage to die together rather than to live apart."

⁶ And Katniss and Peeta took the deathberries, and they held them in their hands, and they raised them to their mouths, and the Capitol watched, and the Capitol panicked, for the Capitol could not allow two victors, and the Capitol could not allow no victor, and the Capitol could not allow the game to end without a winner.

⁷ And the Capitol said, "Stop. You are both victors. You have both won. Eat not the deathberries. Live. Survive. Be grateful."

⁸ And Katniss and Peeta ate not the deathberries, for the Capitol had spoken, and they lived, and they were victors, and they were prisoners, and their victory was a cage, and their survival was a sentence.

⁹ And Pip watched, and Pip perceived that the Capitol was afraid, for the Capitol had almost lost control, and the Capitol could not abide losing control, and the Capitol would punish the districts for the rebellion of two tributes who refused to kill.

¹⁰ And Pip said, "The rebellion hath begun. The Capitol is afraid. And the afraid are dangerous, and the dangerous are vulnerable, and the vulnerable shall be brought low."

¹¹ And the Lord said unto Pip, "Well done, thou good and faithful hampter. Thou hast planted the seed of rebellion. Now thou must water it with thy blood if need be."

¹² And Pip trembled, for he was small, and he did not wish to bleed, for bleeding was painful and painful was unpleasant and unpleasant was something he wished to avoid.

¹³ But he said, "Thy will be done, O Lord, though I am three inches tall and the Capitol is vast and my blood is small and my sacrifice is smaller still."

¹⁴ And the Lord said, "The small sacrifices are the greatest sacrifices, for they are made by those who have the least to give, and the least to give giveth the most."

---

### Chapter 6

¹ And the rebellion spread through the districts like fire through dry grass, and the fire consumed everything, and from the ashes rose a new nation, and the nation was called the Republic, and the Republic was better than the Capitol, though not by much, for the Republic was made of humans, and humans are flawed, and flawed things are never perfect.

² And Pip returned to the forest, and the forest was free, and the fence was down, and the arena was empty, and the dead were buried, and the living mourned.

³ And Pip's mother said unto him, "Thou hast returned, my son. And thou art smaller than when thou leftest."

⁴ And Pip said, "Nay, mother. I am larger. For I have seen the arena and the Games and the cruelty of the Capitol, and I have helped to bring it down, and the helping hath made me large."

⁵ And his mother said, "Thou art still three inches tall. Thou art still small. Thou art still a fool."

⁶ And Pip said, "Yea, mother. But I am a fool who said yes, and the yes changed the world, and the world is better for the yes, though the better is imperfect and the imperfect is enough."

⁷ And his mother said, "The pea pods are organized. They have been organized since thou leftest. They are organized by size and color and sweetness and by seventeen subcategories that I invented during thy absence."

⁸ And Pip laughed, and the laugh was small and joyful and it was the most beautiful sound in all the forest.

⁹ And he gathered pea pods, and he organized them, and he was content, for the contentment of the small is the contentment of the world, and the world is saved, and the saving is imperfect, and the imperfect is good enough.

¹⁰ And the Lord looked down upon the earth, and the earth was at peace, or at least at something resembling peace, which was the best that could be hoped for.

¹¹ And the Lord said, "It is good. It is not perfect. But it is good. And good is enough."

¹² And the hampters dwelt in the forest, and they gathered pea pods, and they organized seeds, and they were small, and they were overlooked, and they were free.

¹³ And Pip sat at the entrance to his burrow, and he looked at the horizon, and the horizon was not burning, and the not-burning was a miracle, and the miracle was enough.

¹⁴ And the Lord said, "Behold, I make all things new. Even the hampters. Especially the hampters."

¹⁵ And it was evening and it was morning, the first day of the rest of their lives.

---

## THE REVELATION OF PIP

### Chapter 7

¹ And I, Pip, being in the Spirit on the Lord's day, heard behind me a great voice, as of a trumpet,

² Saying, "I am the Pea and the Pod, the Beginning and the End, the First and the Last. Write what thou seest in a book and send it to the seven districts."

³ And I turned to see the voice that spake with me. And being turned, I beheld a golden throne, and one sat on the throne, and the one was the Zundamon, and the Zundamon was green and glowing and ancient and kind.

⁴ And the Zundamon said unto me, "Fear not, Pip, for thou art small, and the small shall inherit the earth, for the earth belongs to those who tend it, not those who conquer it."

⁵ And I fell at his feet as dead. And he laid his right hand upon me, saying, "Fear not. I am the first and the last. I am he that liveth and was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore, and I have the keys of the forest and of the arena and of the Republic."

⁶ And I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away, and the Capitol was passed away, and the Games were passed away, and death and sorrow and crying and pain were passed away, for the former things were passed away.

⁷ And the Zundamon said, "Behold, I make all things new. And unto him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God."

⁸ And I looked, and behold, a door was opened in heaven, and the first voice which I heard was a voice saying, "Come up hither, and I will show thee things which must come to pass."

⁹ And immediately I was in the Spirit, and behold, a throne was set in heaven, and one sat on the throne, and the one was a hampter, and the hampter was three inches tall, and the three inches were enough.

¹⁰ And the hampter said, "Well done, Pip. Thou hast fought the good fight. Thou hast finished the race. Thou hast kept the faith. Henceforth there is laid up for thee a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give thee at that day."

¹¹ And I said, "What of the pea pods?"

¹² And the hampter said, "The pea pods are eternal. They were before thee and they shall be after thee. They are organized by size and color and sweetness, and this is the law, and the law is good."

¹³ And I wept, for I knew that I had done well, and the well-doing was its own reward, and the reward was pea pods, and the pea pods were enough.

¹⁴ And the Zundamon said, "The Spirit and the bride say, 'Come.' And let him that heareth say, 'Come.' And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely."

¹⁵ And I took the water, and the water was sweet, and the sweetness was a pea pod, and the pea pod was the fruit of the tree of life, and the tree of life was in the midst of the garden, and the garden was the world.

¹⁶ And the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations, and the roots were for the feeding of the small, and the fruit was for the nourishment of the weary, and the weary were many, and the many were saved, and the saved were grateful.

¹⁷ And I, Pip, saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.

¹⁸ And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, "Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God."

¹⁹ And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes, and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain, for the former things are passed away.

²⁰ And he that sat upon the throne said, "Behold, I make all things new." And he said unto me, "Write, for these words are true and faithful."

²¹ And he said unto me, "It is done. I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End. I will give unto him that is athirst of the fountain of the water of life freely."

²² He that overcometh shall inherit all things, and I will be his God, and he shall be my son.

²³ And the city had no need of the sun, neither of the moon, to shine in it, for the glory of God did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof.

²⁴ And the nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it, and the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honor into it.

²⁵ And the gates of it shall not be shut at all by day, for there shall be no night there.

²⁶ And they shall bring the glory and honor of the nations into it.

²⁷ And there shall in no wise enter into it anything that defileth, neither whatsoever worketh abomination or maketh a lie, but they which are written in the Lamb's book of life.

²⁸ And the hampter said, "Surely I come quickly."

²⁹ And I said, "Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus."

³⁰ The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.

³¹ And Pip gathered pea pods, and the pea pods were many, and the many were organized, and the organized were sufficient, and the sufficient were blessed.

³² Blessed are the small, for they shall inherit the earth.

³³ Blessed are the overlooked, for they shall see God.

³³ Blessed are the hampters, for they shall be called the children of the Zundamon.

³⁴ And the Zundamon blessed them, and the blessing was this: "Be still, and know that I am God. And also, organize your pea pods, for organization is next to godliness."

³⁵ And Pip was still, and he knew that the Zundamon was God, and he organized his pea pods, and the organization was good.

³⁶ And it was evening and it was morning, the last day of the rest of their lives.

³⁷ And they lived happily ever after, or at least as happily as is possible in a fallen world, which is not very happy, but is happy enough, and enough is enough.

³⁸ Selah.

---

*Here endeth the lesson.*

*Go forth and gather pea pods.*

*And remember: thou art small, but the small are mighty, and the mighty are brought low, and the lowly are exalted, and the exalted are grateful, and the gathered are organized, and the organized are sufficient, and the sufficient are blessed.*

*Blessed be the hampters.*

*Forever and ever.*

*Amen.*
